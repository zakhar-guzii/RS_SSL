❯ do we have the same 40% masking here?                                         

⏺ No — the DINOv2 script doesn't mask 40% of the signal. It uses a different
  mechanism entirely:

  - data2vec: sees the full signal, then zeros out 40% of timesteps (MASK_RATIO 
  = 0.40) and predicts the teacher's representation of the masked parts.
  - DINOv2 script: no masking at all. Instead it uses multi-crop — student sees
  random temporal sub-windows (global crops ≈60–100% of length, local crops
  ≈20–50%), teacher sees only the global crops. The self-distillation loss
  compares softmax distributions across these different-length crops, not
  masked-vs-full.

  This matches vanilla DINO. The one thing I explicitly left out (noted in the
  docstring) is DINOv2's actual masking component — it also uses an iBOT-style 
  patch masking loss (mask random patches/timesteps, predict teacher's patch
  tokens there) in addition to the multi-crop loss. I skipped it for
  simplicity/scope.

  Want me to add that iBOT-style masked-patch loss so it's closer to the real
  DINOv2 (and more directly comparable to data2vec's masking)? It'd mean masking
  ~40% of timesteps on one of the global crops and adding a second loss term
  for those masked positions.

  on top of those features.

❯ what other architectures could i try here that might achieve the goal?        

⏺ A few candidates, roughly in order of how well-suited they are to sensor
  time-series (vs. image-inspired methods like DINOv2 which need a modality
  bridge):

  - TS-TCC / TS2Vec (contrastive, purpose-built for time series): augment each
  window twice (jitter/scaling/permutation/time-warp), pull augmented pairs
  together and push other samples apart via InfoNCE. These are the standard
  baselines in HAR SSL literature and typically outperform vision-derived
  methods here since the augmentations are designed for sensor signals, not
  pixels.
  - SimSiam/BYOL: same student/teacher skeleton you already have (encoder + EMA
  or stop-gradient), but drops data2vec's masking and DINO's
  multi-crop/centering for a simpler cosine-similarity loss between two
  augmented views — easiest incremental change from your current code.
  - Masked Autoencoder (MAE): mask 40% of timesteps like data2vec, but
  reconstruct raw values directly (no teacher network) — simpler than data2vec,
  worth trying as a lighter ablation.
  - Wav2Vec2-style contrastive + quantization: closest in spirit to
  accelerometer/audio-like signals, but heavier to implement (needs a codebook).

  My pick: TS-TCC-style contrastive is the strongest bet for actually hitting
  your F1 ≥ 0.925 goal, since it's the closest match to your data modality and
  has published HAR results in that range. Main tradeoff vs. what you have now:
  it needs augmentation design (jitter/scale/permute/warp) rather than masking
  or cropping, and negative-pair batching, but it's less architecturally complex
  than DINOv2's multi-crop + centering machinery.

  Want me to implement TS-TCC-style contrastive SSL as a third script, reusing
  the same encoder/eval harness?

  InfoNCE — it has no negative pairs and no contrastive term at all. InfoNCE
