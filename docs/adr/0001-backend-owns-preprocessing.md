# Backend owns all preprocessing

The App sends a Recording as raw, timestamped 6-channel samples — accelerometer `(ax, ay, az)` + gyroscope `(gx, gy, gz)` — plus a single `units` field for the accelerometer (`"g"` or `"m/s2"` — Android reports m/s², iOS reports g; gyroscope is always rad/s and never converted); it does no resampling, windowing, or normalization of its own. The Backend owns the entire pipeline: accelerometer unit conversion, gap-splitting, 50 Hz resampling, windowing, and per-bundle normalization, exactly mirroring the merge pipeline (`src/data_merge.py`) that produced the training data.

The alternative was to push some of this onto the client — e.g. have the App resample to 50 Hz before sending. We rejected it: every future client (the current App, any later web or CLI client) would have to reimplement the merge pipeline's `resample_interp`/`sliding_windows`/`windows_from_ts` bit-for-bit or silently drift from what the models were trained on, defeating the point of Model Bundles persisting exact training-time norm stats. Keeping preprocessing server-side means a client only has to get one thing right — `units` — and everything else is centrally correct by construction.

The trade-off: Recordings travel over Wi-Fi as full raw sample streams rather than pre-windowed data, which is more bandwidth for a ~15 s capture than necessary — acceptable at MVP scale (shared Wi-Fi, single demo device).
